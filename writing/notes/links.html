Links

A link is both language and an executable relationship.

A link has a rare double life. It is part of a sentence, carrying grammatical and conceptual meaning, and it is also an action that changes the reader’s location. Good linked writing respects both roles.

Link text should make the relationship legible before activation. “The limits of attention” tells more than “read more.” Surrounding prose can indicate whether the destination defines, disputes, extends, or merely neighbors the present idea.

Because links invite departure, their visual treatment requires balance. Too faint and the structure of hypertext disappears; too loud and sustained reading becomes a field of competing exits. Their appearance is a problem of contrast and rhythm.

Links produce the web as an experienced structure. Each click chooses one edge from the graph. A spatial stack can remember those chosen edges as history, leaving the reader’s actual path visible without demanding an overview of every possible path.
