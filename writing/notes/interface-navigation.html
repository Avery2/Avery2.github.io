Interface navigation

Navigation is the design of continuity between states.

AI-generated exampleThis is substantive prototype content written to test the linked-reading interface, not Avery’s published writing.

Navigation is commonly represented by controls: menus, tabs, links, back buttons. Those controls matter, but navigation is the larger experience of moving between states while maintaining a useful model of where one is.

A transition can preserve continuity through language, position, or motion. The destination title repeats the link’s promise. A previous surface remains exposed. A modest animation shows which object arrived and which receded. These are not decorations; they are evidence about causality.

When navigation hides its history, the reader pays with memory. When it displays every possibility equally, the reader pays with attention. A spatial path offers a middle ground: the active route stays visible while the larger information architecture remains latent.

This approach belongs to hypertext, but it also borrows from wayfinding and spatial memory. The route is not the territory. It is the particular sequence this reader has made through it.
