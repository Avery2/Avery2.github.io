Seeing information

Reading is partly the art of deciding what deserves to become visible.

AI-generated exampleThis is substantive prototype content written to test the linked-reading interface, not Avery’s published writing.

Information is not visible merely because it is present. A page can contain every relevant fact and still keep its structure hidden. To see information is to notice distinctions: foreground and background, route and landmark, signal and atmosphere.

That makes design less like decorating a container and more like arranging conditions for perception. Contrast separates; visual hierarchy orders; attention selects. None of them works alone. Strong contrast without order produces noise, while clear hierarchy without enough contrast remains theoretical.

The same problem appears at different scales. A letterform must survive the eye’s limits. A map must turn territory into choices. A link must be visible enough to suggest another direction without dismantling the sentence around it. Each is a proposal about what the reader should be able to notice next.

Good information design does not remove complexity. It gives complexity perceptible shape.

This collection follows that idea across color, typography, wayfinding, and spatial interfaces. The links are not a taxonomy. They are trails between related ways of seeing.
