Cognitive load

An interface spends cognition whenever it asks the reader to hold hidden state.

Cognitive load is not a synonym for “too much on screen.” Removing visible context can increase effort if the reader must remember what disappeared. Complexity can be carried by the display or transferred into memory.

Stable landmarks, consistent typography, and visible history externalize state. They leave more capacity for reading and judgment. The goal is not minimal information; it is minimal unnecessary remembering.
